Intentions

5 minutes

Learning objectives

At the end of the lesson, you should be able to:

  • Describe a food chain and explain how it shows energy flow.
  • Identify the five trophic levels in the sample food chain.
  • Differentiate a food chain from a food web.
  • Construct a simple food chain in the correct sequence.
Think about it: What might happen to a frog if there were no grasshoppers in its habitat?

6. Student challenge: Who eats whom?

Explore the roles of organisms in the sample food chain. An organism can be both a predator and prey, depending on the relationship.

Grasshopper

Is it a predator or prey in this chain?

Frog

The frog eats the grasshopper and is eaten by the snake. Which roles apply?

Snake

The snake eats the frog and is eaten by the hawk.

7. Ecosystem detective: What might happen?

Choose a change in the sample food chain. Predict which organism may be affected based on the feeding relationship.

Select a situation and explain your prediction before revealing the clue.

8. Food chain error hunt

One sequence below does not follow the sample feeding relationships. Find the incorrect one.

9. Create your own food chain

Think of organisms you have observed in a garden, farm, pond, or another habitat. Write a possible food chain and label the producer and consumers. Be ready to explain your choices to a classmate.

Key vocabulary

Trophic level

A stage in a food chain.

Consumer

A living organism in a food chain.

Predator

An animal that eats another animal.

Prey

An animal that is eaten by a predator.

Habitat

The place where an organism lives.

Producer

An organism, usually a green plant or algae, that makes its own food through photosynthesis.
